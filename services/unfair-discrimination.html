Unfair Discrimination

Advice and support on unfair discrimination in the workplace, for employees who have been treated unfairly and for employers responding to a claim.

Book a Consultation Call 011 219 5025

Section 6 of the Employment Equity Act prohibits unfair discrimination against an employee in any employment policy or practice, directly or indirectly, on grounds such as race, gender, sex, pregnancy, marital status, ethnic or social origin, colour, sexual orientation, age, disability, religion, HIV status, conscience, belief, political opinion, culture, language or birth. Harassment is a form of unfair discrimination, and paying different wages for the same work or work of equal value on a listed ground is also prohibited.

A dismissal for a discriminatory reason is automatically unfair under the Labour Relations Act. We help employees understand whether they have a claim and how to pursue it, and help employers respond to allegations and put fair policies in place.

How It Works

  1. Assessment

    We look at what happened and whether a listed or arbitrary ground is involved.

  2. Internal steps

    Where suitable, a grievance or complaint is raised in the workplace first.

  3. Referral

    The dispute is referred to the CCMA within the time limit.

  4. Resolution

    Conciliation, then arbitration or the Labour Court, depending on the claim.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do I have to refer an unfair discrimination dispute?

Generally within six months of the act or omission complained of. A late referral needs a condonation application.

Where is an unfair discrimination dispute decided?

It is first referred to the CCMA for conciliation. If it is not resolved, it may be arbitrated at the CCMA in certain cases, such as sexual harassment claims, claims by employees earning below the earnings threshold, or where the parties agree. Otherwise it goes to the Labour Court.

Is every difference in treatment unfair discrimination?

No. Differentiation can be fair, for example affirmative action measures consistent with the Employment Equity Act, or distinctions based on the inherent requirements of a job. Whether it is unfair depends on the facts.
