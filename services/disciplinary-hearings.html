Disciplinary Hearings: Procedure, Charges & Representation

Fair, professional support for workplace disciplinary processes, from the first investigation to chairing the hearing and deciding the outcome.

Book a Consultation Call 011 219 5025

Under the Labour Relations Act and its Code of Good Practice: Dismissal, a dismissal for misconduct or poor performance must be both substantively fair (there is a valid reason) and procedurally fair (a fair process was followed). The employee must be told of the allegations, given reasonable time to prepare, allowed to respond and to be represented by a fellow employee or union representative, and informed of the outcome and the reasons for it.

Labour Law Alliance can act as an independent chairperson, help employers prepare and present their case as initiator, or advise employees who are facing a hearing.

How We Can Help

  • Investigating allegations and advising whether there is a case to answer
  • Drafting charges and the notice to attend a disciplinary hearing
  • Independent chairing of disciplinary and incapacity hearings
  • Acting as initiator to present the employer’s case
  • Poor work performance and ill-health incapacity processes
  • Advising on an appropriate sanction, from a warning to dismissal
  • Appeal hearings, and preparing for a possible CCMA referral afterwards
  • Guiding employees on their rights before and during a hearing

Who We Help

We help you run a disciplinary process that is fair, consistent and properly documented, so the outcome is far more likely to stand if it is taken to the CCMA.

Employees

How It Works

  1. Investigation

    Gathering facts, statements and evidence before any charges are laid.

  2. Notice & charges

    Clear charges and proper notice, giving the employee time to prepare.

  3. The hearing

    Both sides present evidence and arguments before an impartial chairperson.

  4. Outcome & sanction

    A reasoned finding and a fair, consistent sanction, in writing.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much notice must an employee get before a hearing?

The law does not set a fixed period, but the employee must have a reasonable time to prepare. Many disciplinary codes provide for at least 48 hours, and complex matters may need longer. Check what your own code says.

Can an employee bring a lawyer to a disciplinary hearing?

Usually representation is limited to a fellow employee or a shop steward. Legal representation is only allowed if the disciplinary code provides for it or the chairperson permits it, for example in a complex matter.

Why use an independent chairperson?

An outside chairperson reduces the risk of bias claims and helps make sure the process and outcome can stand up if the matter is later referred to the CCMA.
