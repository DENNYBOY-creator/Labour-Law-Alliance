Performance Management

Fair, structured performance management, from setting clear standards to handling poor work performance, probation and incapacity correctly.

Book a Consultation → Call 011 219 5025

Under the Code of Good Practice: Dismissal, an employee who is not performing must be told what standard is required, given fair evaluation, guidance, training or counselling, and a reasonable time to improve before dismissal is considered. Probationary employees must also be given proper assessment and support.

Labour Law Alliance helps employers build simple, fair performance processes and manage poor performance, absenteeism and incapacity in a way that is lawful and gives employees a real chance to improve.

How It Works

  1. 01

    Standards

    Clear, measurable expectations that the employee understands.

  2. 02

    Assessment

    Regular, documented reviews against those standards.

  3. 03

    Support

    Guidance, training or counselling, and a reasonable time to improve.

  4. 04

    Next steps

    Improvement is recognised; if not, a fair incapacity process follows.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can we dismiss an employee for poor performance?

Yes, but only after a fair process. The employee must know the required standard, be given guidance and a reasonable opportunity to improve, and the employer must consider alternatives before dismissal.

How is probation different?

Probation lets an employer assess a new employee’s performance before confirming the appointment. The employee must still receive evaluation, guidance and support, and the period should be reasonable for the job.

Is poor performance a disciplinary matter?

Usually not. Poor performance is dealt with as incapacity, through counselling and improvement plans. Misconduct, such as deliberately refusing to work, is handled through the disciplinary process.
