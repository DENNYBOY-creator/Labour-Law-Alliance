What Counts as an Unfair Labour Practice?

Not every unfair act at work is an "unfair labour practice" in law. Here is what the Labour Relations Act covers and how to raise a dispute.

Discipline & Disputes · 27 November 2026 · 2 min read · By Labour Law Alliance

Many employees feel they have been treated unfairly at work, but in law an unfair labour practice has a specific meaning. Section 186(2) of the Labour Relations Act limits it to certain kinds of unfair conduct by an employer towards an employee. Knowing whether your complaint fits within the definition is the first step.

What the Labour Relations Act covers

An unfair labour practice is any unfair act or omission between an employer and an employee involving:

  • Promotion or demotion: for example, an unfair process or irrational decision when filling a post.
  • Probation: unfair conduct relating to probation, other than a dismissal.
  • Training: unfairly denying an employee training.
  • Benefits: unfair conduct relating to the provision of benefits.
  • Suspension or discipline short of dismissal: such as an unfair suspension or an unfair final written warning.
  • Re-employment: failing or refusing to reinstate or re-employ a former employee under an agreement.
  • Occupational detriment for making a protected disclosure under the Protected Disclosures Act.

What it does not cover

Dismissals are dealt with separately as unfair dismissal disputes. General grievances that fall outside the list above, and disputes about new terms such as annual wage increases (disputes of interest), are usually not unfair labour practices. These may need a grievance, collective bargaining or another route instead.

How to refer a dispute

An unfair labour practice dispute must generally be referred to the CCMA or the relevant bargaining council within 90 days of the act or omission, or of the date you became aware of it. The matter goes to conciliation first and, if unresolved, to arbitration.

The employee usually has to show that the conduct happened and that it was unfair. If it is found to be an unfair labour practice, the arbitrator can order an appropriate remedy, which may include compensation of up to 12 months’ remuneration or, in some cases, putting the employee in the position they should have been in.

Before you refer

  • Raise the issue through the internal grievance procedure where possible, and keep records.
  • Gather documents such as the job advert, interview scores, policies or warning letters.
  • Do not let the 90 days run out while waiting for an internal process to finish.
