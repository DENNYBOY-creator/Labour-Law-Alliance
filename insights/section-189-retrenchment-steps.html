Section 189 Retrenchments: The Steps Employers Must Follow

Retrenchments must follow a strict process under the Labour Relations Act. These are the key steps, from the section 189(3) notice to severance pay.

People & Relations · 27 November 2026 · 3 min read · By Labour Law Alliance

When a business needs to cut jobs for operational reasons (economic, technological, structural or similar needs), section 189 of the Labour Relations Act sets out the process the employer must follow. A retrenchment can be found unfair if there was no valid operational reason or if the process was not followed properly.

1. Contemplating retrenchment

The duty to consult starts when the employer contemplates dismissals for operational requirements, not after a final decision has been made. Consultation must happen while there is still a genuine chance to influence the outcome.

2. Identify who to consult

Section 189(1) sets out who the employer must consult, in order: anyone it must consult under a collective agreement; a workplace forum; a registered trade union whose members are likely to be affected; or, if none of these apply, the affected employees or their nominated representatives.

3. Issue the section 189(3) notice

The employer must invite the consulting parties to consult in writing and disclose relevant information, including:

  • the reasons for the proposed retrenchment;
  • the alternatives considered and why they were rejected;
  • the number of employees likely to be affected and their job categories;
  • the proposed selection criteria;
  • when the dismissals are likely to take effect;
  • the proposed severance pay and other assistance;
  • the possibility of future re-employment.

4. Meaningful consultation

The parties must engage in a meaningful joint consensus-seeking process on ways to avoid or reduce dismissals, change their timing, soften their effects, the selection criteria and severance pay. The employer must consider the representations made and, where they are made in writing, respond in writing.

5. Fair selection

Employees must be selected using criteria that have been agreed or, if none were agreed, criteria that are fair and objective. Last in, first out (LIFO) is common, sometimes adjusted to retain necessary skills.

6. Notice and severance pay

Affected employees must receive notice of termination, and severance pay of at least one week’s remuneration for each completed year of continuous service under section 41 of the Basic Conditions of Employment Act, plus any outstanding leave pay and notice pay.

Large-scale retrenchments: section 189A

Employers with more than 50 employees who plan to retrench above certain thresholds within a 12-month period must also follow section 189A. This adds the option of a CCMA facilitator and longer timelines: notice of termination generally cannot be given until at least 60 days after the section 189(3) notice.

When things go wrong

Employees who believe a retrenchment was unfair can generally refer a dispute within 30 days of dismissal. Getting advice before issuing the section 189(3) notice is far cheaper than defending a flawed process later.
