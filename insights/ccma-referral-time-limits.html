How Long Do I Have to Refer a Dismissal Dispute to the CCMA?

Strict time limits apply to CCMA referrals. Here is how long you have, how to refer your dispute and what happens if you miss the deadline.

Book a Consultation Call 011 219 5025

Discipline & Disputes · 27 November 2026 · 3 min read · By Labour Law Alliance

If you believe you were unfairly dismissed or treated unfairly at work, the Labour Relations Act gives you the right to refer a dispute to the Commission for Conciliation, Mediation and Arbitration (CCMA), or to a bargaining council if one covers your sector. But that right comes with strict time limits, and missing them is one of the most common reasons good cases never get heard.

The main time limits

  • Unfair dismissal: 30 days. The dispute must generally be referred within 30 days of the date of dismissal or, where relevant, the date the employer made a final decision to dismiss or to uphold the dismissal.
  • Unfair labour practice: 90 days. The dispute must be referred within 90 days of the act or omission complained of or, if later, the date you became aware of it.
  • Unfair discrimination: six months. Disputes under the Employment Equity Act must generally be referred within six months of the act or omission.

These periods are counted in calendar days, so weekends and public holidays are included. Do not wait until the last few days.

How to refer a dispute

A dispute is referred by completing the CCMA referral form (LRA Form 7.11) and submitting it to the CCMA or the relevant bargaining council, either online, by email or at an office. You must also serve a copy on the other party, usually your employer, and keep proof that you did so. Referring a dispute to the CCMA is free.

Make sure the form clearly states what kind of dispute it is, the date of dismissal or of the unfair conduct, and the outcome you want, such as reinstatement or compensation.

What if I am late?

A late referral is not automatically the end of the matter, but you must apply for condonation. The commissioner will generally consider:

  • how late the referral is;
  • your explanation for the delay;
  • your prospects of success; and
  • any prejudice to the other party.

Condonation is not guaranteed, and a weak explanation can sink an otherwise strong case. The safest approach is always to refer on time.

What happens after the referral?

The CCMA will schedule a conciliation meeting, where a commissioner helps the parties try to reach a settlement. If the dispute is not resolved, the commissioner issues a certificate of outcome. Depending on the type of dispute, you may then request arbitration, where a commissioner hears evidence and makes a binding award, or the matter may need to go to the Labour Court. A request for arbitration also has a time limit, generally 90 days from the date the certificate is issued.

Practical tips

  • Write down the exact date of your dismissal and keep your dismissal letter or outcome.
  • An internal appeal does not necessarily stop the clock. If in doubt, refer within 30 days of the original dismissal.
  • Keep copies of everything you submit and serve.
